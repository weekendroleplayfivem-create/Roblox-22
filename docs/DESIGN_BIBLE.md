# Depths — Design Bible

Living design doc. Update this alongside code changes — it's what keeps a
build this large from feeling stitched-together as biomes 2 and 3, the full
UI suite, and the economy layer get built out on top of the Phase 1
architecture scaffold.

**Status:** Phase 1 (architecture scaffold + data-driven content stubs) complete.
Biome 1 (Crypt) vertical slice — instanced geometry, movement/camera, live
combat, boss fight — is not yet built; see `README.md` for why and what that
means for next steps.

---

## 1. Visual & mechanical identity

"Torchlit stone and old brass" is the base UI/world language (see
`UIStyle.lua`): warm dark stone surfaces, oxidized-brass interactive accents,
serif display type for headers. Every biome tints that base rather than
replacing it, so the hub, HUD, and menus read as one game across all three
biomes instead of three re-skinned templates.

## 2. Biomes

### The Crypt (biome 1)
A flooded ossuary-cathedral built for a court that outlived its welcome.
Cold and funerary, not jump-scare hostile — the danger is patient. Mechanical
hook: **Chill-stacking control** (3 stacks of Chill hard-Stuns), rewarding
deliberate engagement over rushing rooms. Generated with **BSP** — the Crypt
is planned architecture that partially collapsed, so room-and-corridor
generation with irregular sizing and loop-back corridors reads truer than an
organic cave algorithm would. Palette: wet grey-blue stone, guttering
white-blue torchlight. Audio: distant choir-drone ambience that swells on
combat engage.

### The Fungal Depths (biome 2)
A cave network consumed by one ancient mycelial organism, still faintly
aware of what walks through it. Claustrophobic and ambush-driven. Mechanical
hook: **read-and-react** — camouflaged ambushers, forced-kite bombers, and
area-denial spore bursts punish players who don't slow down. Generated with
**cellular automata** — no original floor plan exists here, so automata-
carved irregular caverns (with a flood-fill pass to guarantee full
traversability) read truer than planned rooms. Palette: bioluminescent
green-violet spore light against wet black stone. Audio: low fungal-drone
hum punctuated by spore-pop tells (critical for the ambush-read gameplay).

### The Molten Forge (biome 3)
An industrial forge-complex left running on its own momentum long after the
last smith died. Aggressive and loud. Mechanical hook: **terrain as
combatant** — lava pools and collapsing floor sections generated by a
hazard-grid pass mean positioning against the room matters as much as
positioning against the enemy. Generated with **BSP + a hazard-grid pass** —
still planned industrial spaces, with a second pass marking a
`hazardDensity`-controlled fraction of floor tiles as active hazards.
Palette: rust-orange and cooling-slag black against hot-metal glow. Audio:
rhythmic distant hammering that syncs with the combat music layer.

**Why three different algorithms instead of one reused across biomes:** a
uniform grid-of-rooms (or a single generator reskinned three times) is the
single most common tell of an AI-generated Roblox dungeon. Each biome's
*setting* implies a different generation logic — the Crypt has an architect,
the Fungal Depths never did, the Molten Forge has both a floor plan and an
active hazard — so the generation choice is load-bearing for the fiction,
not cosmetic.

## 3. Enemy roster

| Archetype | Biome | Mechanical hook |
|---|---|---|
| Grave Warden | Crypt | Bone shield blocks frontal damage — must be flanked |
| Wailing Husk | Crypt | Channels a dread bolt that must be interrupted or it applies fear |
| Rattling Crawler | Crypt | Swarm; burrows under standing players — punishes planting |
| Spore Stalker | Fungal Depths | Camouflaged until a spore-puff telegraphs its lunge |
| Bloat Cyst | Fungal Depths | Suicide bomber; must be killed at range or kited |
| Mycelial Weaver | Fungal Depths | Roots the player; must be interrupted or line-of-sight broken |
| Slag Brute | Molten Forge | Frontal armor plate; slam can be sidestepped into hazard terrain |
| Cinder Wisp | Molten Forge | Fast swarm-flier; drops burning ember trail, forces movement |
| Forgebound Sentinel | Molten Forge | Stationary turret-caster; must be interrupted before barrage |

## 4. Bosses

**The Sepulcher King** (Crypt) — 3 phases. P1 teaches Chill-stacking. P2
(`AddWave`) summons two Grave Wardens and periodically freezes the floor. P3
(`EnrageTimer`) sheds its shield entirely (fully flankable) but ramps attack
speed on a visible timer — a race, not an attrition check.

**The Mother Spore** (Fungal Depths) — 3 phases. P1 teaches spore-burst lane
reading. P2 (`ArenaCollapse`) dims the arena and the boss camouflages between
bursts — the hardest "readable chaos" test case in the game, tracked by audio
tell alone. P3 (`EnrageTimer`) shrinks the safe zone over time.

**The Forgeheart Colossus** (Molten Forge) — 3 phases. P1 teaches the
sidestep-into-hazard counterplay shared with Slag Brutes. P2
(`ArenaCollapse`) ignites two-thirds of the floor into rotating hazard tiles.
P3 exposes a critical-damage weak point but doubles attack tempo — a
high-risk burn window.

Full phase data lives in `ReplicatedStorage/Shared/Data/Bosses.lua`.

## 5. Itemization & rarity

Five tiers: Common → Uncommon → Rare → Epic → Legendary (`Data/Rarity.lua`).
Rarity is **not** a flat stat multiplier — each tier past Common adds
`affixSlots` (0/1/2/3/4), so a Legendary reads as categorically different
from a scaled-up Common rather than the same item with bigger numbers.
Legendary items additionally carry a unique named identity and flavor text
tied to the game's lore (e.g. `OathbreakersGrief`, `ForgeheartEmber`) rather
than a generic "[Rarity] [Base Name]" template.

Representative examples per weapon category (`Data/Items/Weapons.lua`):
- **MeleeArc:** Rusted Shortsword (Common) → Oathbreaker's Grief (Legendary)
- **MeleeThrust:** Warden Spike (Common) → The Last Rite (Legendary)
- **Ranged:** Sporebone Pistol (Common) → Mother's Last Breath (Legendary)
- **Magic:** Cinder-Touched Focus (Common) → Forgeheart Ember (Legendary)

## 6. Talent tree & relic pool

Two build-defining systems, deliberately separate:

- **Talents** (`Data/Talents.lua`) — permanent, account-scoped, unlocked in
  the hub with Soft currency. A real tree (4 tiers, prerequisites), not a
  flat list — 3 Tier-1 foundation nodes feed into 4 archetype-leaning Tier-2
  nodes, each with a Tier-3 deepening node, with one Tier-4 capstone
  authored so far (`CrimsonHarvest`).
- **Relics** (`Data/Relics.lua`) — per-run, Hades/Balatro-style picks offered
  3-at-a-time at every floor transition. 18 relics across 4 build
  archetypes (**Bleed**, **Kiter**, **Bulwark**, **Pyromancer**) plus a small
  universal pool — well above the "5 relics" floor that reads as an
  unfinished system. Each relic lists `synergiesWith` so the run-readout HUD
  can show *why* a build is strong, and `LootService.RollRelicOffer` biases
  offers toward archetype tags the player has already invested in, so a
  run's identity strengthens rather than being random noise floor-to-floor.

**Archetype synergy map:**
- **Bleed** (Festering Hook → Crimson Ledger → Wounds That Count → The
  Patient Knife capstone-tier execute): stack Bleed from weapon variety,
  scale its damage with stack count, and finish low-health targets instantly.
- **Kiter** (Frostbound Lantern → Last Steps Seen → The Long Retreat →
  Winter's Audience): apply Chill on hit, dash more often, deal more damage
  at range, and burst a fully-Chilled (stunned) target for True damage.
- **Bulwark** (Warden's Oath → Unbroken Line → The Last Guard → Slagbound
  Bastion): reflect blocked damage, hold the block window longer, survive a
  killing blow once per run, and AoE-stagger on a perfect block.
- **Pyromancer** (Cinder Seed → Forgebreath Sigil → Ash to Ash →
  Forgeheart's Promise): apply Burn on cast, ramp Burn damage on consecutive
  casts, spread Burn on kill, and cast for free while a target burns.

## 7. System-design rationale ("why" log)

- **Why BSP/CA/HazardGrid per biome, not one algorithm:** see §2 above —
  generation choice is tied to each biome's fiction, and using one algorithm
  everywhere is the clearest AI-slop tell in the genre right now.
- **Why difficulty scales by *absolute* floor number, not reset per biome**
  (`BiomeRegistry.GetDifficultyMultiplier`): biome transitions should feel
  like a continuation of the same descent, not three separate difficulty
  curves stapled together.
- **Why the ante/pact modifier system is a flat risk/reward multiplier, not
  a separate unlock track** (`EconomyService.AnteModifiers`): keeps
  difficulty selection a pre-run choice that reads on the loot/enemy tables
  directly, rather than gating content behind grind.
- **Why cosmetic/convenience purchases are structurally separated from
  Soft-currency (talent/gear) spending** (`EconomyService` module doc): the
  "not pay-to-win" boundary is enforced by the shop catalog's type system,
  not by policy alone — a shop entry referencing a talent/relic/item id is a
  type error, not just a design-review catch.
- **Why PartyService exists even though launch is solo-first:** every other
  service keys per-player state through `Get()`-style accessors instead of
  direct table indexing specifically so switching those tables to be
  party-keyed later is a config flag, not a rewrite.
- **Why status effects are one generic data-driven table
  (`StatusEffects.lua`) instead of per-enemy one-off hacks:** a new biome's
  signature debuff (e.g. a Season 2 "Corrode") should be addable as one data
  entry plus a tick-handler id, without touching `CombatService` or any
  enemy's bespoke code.

## 8. Open follow-ups (tracked, not hidden)

- Full per-floor loot tables (currently `LootService` resolves against the
  entire weapon/armor pool by rarity as a functional placeholder).
- Equipped-weapon-driven combat damage (`CombatService._resolveEquippedWeaponDamage`
  is a fixed placeholder pending `InventoryService` wiring into the attack path).
- Boss arena vault models, add-wave spawning, and arena-collapse hazard
  toggling (`BossService._spawnPhaseAdds`/`_collapseArena`) — depend on
  hand-placed vault `Model`s that must be authored in Studio.
- The full HUD/menu suite, itemization content breadth, and biomes 2–3
  geometry — scoped as later phases per `README.md`.
